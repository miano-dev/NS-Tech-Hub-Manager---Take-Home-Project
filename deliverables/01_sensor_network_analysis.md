# Sensor Network Challenge: six-week network assessment

**Project window:** 2 March-12 April 2026 (42 days)  
**Time basis:** source telemetry is UTC; site maintenance dates are local East Africa Time (EAT, UTC+3).  
**Source files:** `data/raw/`  
**Reproducible analysis:** `src/analyze.py`; generated tables: `outputs/`.

## Executive assessment

The findings shows that the network has both device faults and shared infrastructure failures. The most consequential system-level incident is GW-02's 54-hour cellular-backhaul disconnection. Its local log counted 1,742 messages during that interval, but only 11 detailed GW-02 reception rows appear in `telemetry.csv`. This is evidence of a data-forwarding/collection gap, but not proof that the sensors stopped transmitting. GW-01 then shows recurring overnight outages from 29 March, with a minimum reported 12 V-system battery reading of 11.30 V; the maintenance log independently records the gateway found off on 2 April.

Several device-level faults remain even after those network problems are separated: SM-05 and SM-07 send hourly rather than every 15 minutes; SM-06 emits an impossible `2000001` value for both soil moisture and temperature; SM-03 has a pronounced battery/charging decline and stops reporting; SM-08 becomes intermittent after elephant damage; C-04 has repeated `(0, 0)` GPS positions; and WS-02 reports no rainfall at all while the other stations record rain. C-06 has an emerging battery/charging risk. WS-03's firmware register is stale relative to the maintenance log.

## Method and assumptions

1. I validated the supplied file descriptions, timestamp range, required columns and device register before interpreting values. All telemetry timestamps were parsed as UTC. The maintenance log is local time, so its dates were compared with UTC after applying the stated +3-hour offset where timing mattered.
2. `telemetry.csv` is one row per message **per receiving gateway**. I therefore kept the raw reception table for gateway signal comparisons and deduplicated uplink events on `(device\\\_id, message\\\_counter)` for reporting-rate and completeness calculations. The supplied counters do not appear to wrap during this six-week sample. A production idempotency key should also include the device session/reset identity and a bounded timestamp window.
3. Expected counts over 42 days are 4,032 for each 15-minute sensor and 1,008 for each hourly collar. Completeness below is deduplicated uplinks divided by these schedule opportunities. This is an operational completeness measure, not a claim that every missed scheduled reading was definitely transmitted.
4. I separated a device fault from a shared fault by looking for coincident gaps across devices, gateway status/backhaul, gateway-local message counts, duplicated reception paths, RSSI/SNR, battery/solar data and dated maintenance notes. A plausible cause is labelled as a hypothesis where the files do not prove it.

The raw export contains **64,773 reception rows**. Deduplication leaves **47,035 uplink events** and removes **17,738 extra gateway receptions**. Removing those duplicate receptions is essential: otherwise nodes heard by both gateways appear to report more often than they actually do.

### Devices and paths that look broadly healthy

**SM-04, SM-06's** radio/reporting path, **WL-01, WL-02, WS-02's** temperature path, WS-03's data path and the collar reporting cadence are generally strong. Their specific sensor-quality exceptions are described above. **SM-04** and both water-level devices are heard by both gateways often enough to provide useful reception diversity. A node heard by two gateways should be counted once for reporting completeness, but both receptions should be retained for RF diagnostics.

## 2.How I separated device problems from network problems

* **Shared time window plus gateway-local counter:** the GW-02 backhaul outage affects many devices at once. During the same period, the status log says the gateway was online and receiving dozens of messages per hour, while detailed packet rows disappear from the export. This isolates the failure after LoRa reception: the backhaul/forwarding/collection path.
* **Gateway-specific outages plus alternate reception:** the GW-01 offline pattern is shared by devices that rely on it. Devices also heard by GW-02 can continue to appear in telemetry, which is why a single gateway reception should not be confused with a device transmission.
* **Device-local signatures:** SM-06 keeps its schedule but emits a sentinel; SM-05/07 keep stable power but transmit at the wrong cadence; C-04 transmits but has invalid coordinates; WS-02 transmits but rainfall is flat. These cannot be explained by a general gateway outage.
* **Physical maintenance correlation:** the SM-08 and SM-06 dates align closely with damage/digging notes. These are strong leads, but a technician still needs to inspect them.

## 3.Daily automatic checks and alert routing



|Check|Rule / threshold|Notify / response|
|-|-|-|
|Missing reports|Alert when 3 consecutive expected intervals are missed; critical if no sensor uplink for 2 hours or no collar uplink for 3 hours. for example, daily completeness below 90% is a warning; below 75% is high.|Tech Hub Manager; assigned field technician for device-level incidents.|
|Backhaul and ingestion|Gateway offline for more than 1 heartbeat; backhaul down for 15 minutes; local messages received exceed detailed/forwarded messages by more than 5% over 15 minutes; oldest queued uplink age exceeds 30 minutes.|Network/on-call technician and Tech Hub Manager. Escalate at 45 minutes because the requirement is delivery within one hour.|
|End-to-end latency|`ingested\\\_at - measured\\\_at` above 45 minutes (warning) or 60 minutes (breach). Track p50, p95 and max by gateway.|Tech Hub Manager/platform on-call.|
|Battery and solar|Per-model voltage thresholds; warn below vendor low-battery threshold or on a sustained decline greater than 0.05 V/week. Flag no daytime charge current over 3 sunny days when battery is also falling. For a 12 V gateway, alert at the battery manufacturer's low-voltage limit.|Tech Hub Manager, Field technician.|
|Physical value validation|Reject placeholder values or unusable measurements; enforce profile hard ranges (for example soil moisture 0-100% VWC, non-negative rainfall, configured water-level range, valid temperature range). Separate hard rejects from soft outlier warnings.|Data engineer/Tech Hub Manager; field technician if a sensor fault is suspected.|
|Flatline / stuck sensor|Same value for 24 hours or no variation over 96 readings where variation is normally expected. Rainfall zero for 48 hours while nearby stations record rain triggers a check, not an automatic failure.|Instrument owner, Tech-Hub Manager and project data lead.|
|GPS quality|`(0,0)`, invalid fix flag, stale fix, impossible jump/speed, or more than 5% invalid fixes in 24 hours. Retain raw fix status.|Tech Hub Manager, Livestock/collar technician and project lead.|

## 4.Prioritized one-day field visit

**a) First: restore shared infrastructure (morning hours).** At GW-01, inspect and load-test the 12 V battery, measure panel output and charge-controller behaviour, check shade/dirt/tilt, fuse, wiring and ground. Replace the battery or controller only after measurements identify the weak part. At GW-02, inspect SIM/APN/data plan, modem logs, LTE antenna/coax and signal; force a controlled backhaul interruption/recovery and prove that queued messages replay without loss or duplication. Export gateway local logs before rebooting.

**b) Second: restore high-value sensor data.** SM-06: capture raw payload, check decoder version and probe/cable, clean/reseat, take a manual soil reference reading and replace the probe/cable if needed. SM-03: measure charge current and battery under load; repair panel/lead/connector or replace the battery. SM-08: inspect damage and raise/secure the post with a protective cage and cable strain relief. SM-05/07: read back interval and firmware settings; set 15-minute sampling/uplink, test one node, then apply the validated configuration to the second.

**c) Third: validate lower-frequency quality issues.** C-04: verify GNSS fix and antenna placement; check whether zero coordinates are a documented no-fix sentinel. C-06: inspect charging and battery trend. WS-02: clean and test the tipping bucket/reed switch with a measured water test; compare a manual gauge. WS-03: verify actual firmware and update the register.

**Close-out:** run an end-to-end test from at least one fixed node and one collar through the gateway to NS Analytics; verify timestamp, decoded values, deduplication, alert and receipt latency. Photograph installations and record exact parts, serial numbers, firmware, coordinates, measurements and work in the register before leaving.

**Bring:** a tested 12 V deep-cycle replacement battery sized to the installed gateway system; spare charge controller, fuses, solar cable/connectors and weatherproof glands; LTE SIM, known-good router/modem and external antenna/coax; one spare LoRaWAN node and compatible soil probe/cable; small solar panel/battery for sensor testing; GPS collar antenna/strap; tipping-bucket reed switch or replacement module; post, anchors, clamps, animal-resistant guard, cable ties and conduit; multimeter, clamp meter, USB/NFC configuration tools, laptop, handheld soil reference and manual rain gauge. Confirm connector/model compatibility and battery chemistry before loading spares.

## 5.Information Needed for Each Device and Who Is Responsible for Keeping It Updated for automated checks

The tech team should keep a versioned device registry as the source of truth, joined to every uplink by immutable device identity (DevEUI/device ID). Each record should include:

* Identity, device class, model, hardware revision, serial number, firmware and decoder version; installation/commissioning date and current lifecycle state.
* Coordinates (WGS84), elevation, plot/asset, antenna height/orientation, expected and backup gateways, RF baseline (RSSI/SNR), coverage zone and site contact.
* Expected sample and uplink intervals, timezone, permitted reporting window, payload fields, units, scale factors, sentinel codes, hard-valid ranges, warning ranges and calibration date.
* Battery chemistry/model, nominal voltage/capacity, low-battery and critical thresholds, solar panel rating, expected charge window, sleep/sample/transmit configuration and last measured battery/charge current.
* LoRaWAN identity/session, region/channel plan, data rate/ADR policy, transmit power, frame-counter/reset policy, join status, firmware rollout group and last confirmed configuration read-back.
* Owner, criticality, maintenance history, last/next inspection, warranty, spare compatibility, supplier and replacement/RMA history.

The Tech Hub Manager should own the schema and validation rules. Field technicians or tech hub manager should update installation, location, maintenance, photographs and measured configuration at commissioning and every visit. The platform/device or engineer or the tech hub manager should update firmware, payload decoder and network settings through reviewed changes. Every change should be time-stamped, attributable and auditable; firmware/config updates should be staged and rolled back if validation fails. The tech team should not rely on free-text maintenance notes as the only source of operational configuration.
